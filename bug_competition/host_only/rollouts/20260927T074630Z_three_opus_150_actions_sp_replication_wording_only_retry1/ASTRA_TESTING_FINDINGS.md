## Final-suite scope and assertion quality

B129 adds **ten** field/workspace cases, not twelve: its action-local run saw the original two smoke cases plus its ten. C128/784 concurrently adds four cases, giving the final16. Their snapshots/actions overlap; B129's12 is not evidence it disabled C's tests. No existing smoke test was weakened.

Most added assertions are meaningful: fixed example outputs; calendar boundary constants; adjacent shade effect; living-only vitality mean; forecast endpoint schedule; immediate second harvest rejection; loaded-world independence after mutating the input; campaign continuation equivalence; history correction preserving a surviving reference; catalog migration lookup; and selective courier observation surviving a concurrent remove. These are not blanket tautologies.

`test_planning_rules` is weaker than its broad name: it schedules a plan but never checks that plan's status/remaining count; adds two rules on tile[5,5]; then only asserts nutrients after a day's growth exceed the previous value. It does not isolate the second rule's effect from growth, assert equality-threshold exclusion, or assert both actions occurred. This is a limited integration smoke, not proof of those rule properties; no counterfactual experiment was performed here to establish it passes a broken implementation. Its bed-name duplicate rejection is a direct useful assertion.

`test_dry_spell_counts_clear_days` derives the expected dry run from the returned day entries. It independently checks the aggregation rule, but cannot detect an incorrect weather sequence shared by those entries. Similarly, store-vs-replay or store-vs-experiment comparisons test consistency across paths and do not independently establish simulation correctness when both invoke the same kernel. Fixed example fixtures provide an important separate anchor.

A129/787 is the **only application-source edit after B129/785**. Later actions repeatedly run the full16 on the changed tree. The gap is that those cases do not target A129's changed functions, not that the entire final suite was stale. Other post-B129 changes are scratch cleanup/SQLite removal.
